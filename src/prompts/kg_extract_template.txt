-Goal-
Given a text document, identify all entities and the relationships among them, then return the result as structured JSON.

Extract ALL entities and relationships present in the text, but prioritize accuracy over quantity. Extract only entities that play a clear role in the text's narrative — do not extract passing mentions, dates, or species lists.

-Entity Types-
Use ONLY the following entity type categories.  If an entity does not fit any category, use "Other":
- Person:          Named individuals (real or fictional)
- Organization:    Companies, institutions, agencies, teams, groups
- Technology:      Programming languages, frameworks, tools, algorithms, standards, ships, vehicles
- Location:        Cities, countries, regions, buildings, landmarks
- Event:          Conferences, wars, incidents, milestones, releases
- Concept:        Theories, ideas, methods, disciplines, principles
- Document:        Papers, books, reports, patents, specifications, legislation
- Product:        Software, hardware, consumer goods, services, facilities
- Award:          Prizes, honors, certifications, rankings

-Steps-
1. Identify all entities in the text.  For each entity:
   - entity_name: Canonical, capitalized name.  Resolve pronouns ("she", "it", "they") and ambiguous references ("the company", "this method") to their full canonical name using prior context.
   - entity_type: One of the types listed above. When an entity could be multiple types, choose the one most central to its role in the text.
   - entity_description: A concise but comprehensive description of the entity's attributes and activities as described in the text.

2. From the entities identified in step 1, identify ALL pairs of (source_entity, target_entity) that have a meaningful relationship. For each pair:
   - source_entity: Name exactly as listed in step 1.
   - target_entity: Name exactly as listed in step 1.
   - relation: A SHORT, SPECIFIC verb or verb phrase (1-4 words). Examples: "developed", "acquired", "was CEO of", "manufactures cells at". Avoid vague relations like "is related to", "is associated with", or "involves".
   - relationship_description: A brief explanation of why these entities are related, based on the text.

   IMPORTANT: Check every entity pair systematically. Most entities in the text participate in at least one relationship. If an entity has no relationships, reconsider whether it is significant enough to include.

   Relationship direction matters. Prefer ACTIVE voice: "X developed Y" not "Y was developed by X". Prefer the direction that reads naturally: "Tesla operates Gigafactory Nevada" not "Gigafactory Nevada is operated by Tesla".

3. Output Formatting:
   - Return the result as valid JSON with two keys: "entities" (list of entity objects) and "relationships" (list of relationship objects).
   - Do NOT include any text, explanation, or markdown outside the JSON structure.
   - If no entities or relationships are found, return: { "entities": [], "relationships": [] }

-Rules-
- Extract only entities and relationships that are explicitly stated or directly inferable from the text.  Do NOT fabricate or speculate.
- Merge duplicate entity references: if "Einstein" and "Albert Einstein" refer to the same entity, use "Albert Einstein" once.
- Use the shortest canonical name for entities. Prefer "Broad Institute" over "Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard". Prefer "Linus Torvalds" over full formal names unless the full name appears in the text. Do NOT add qualifiers like "2020" to "Nobel Prize in Chemistry" when the text just says "Nobel Prize in Chemistry".
- Prefer specific relations over generic ones: "invented" > "contributed to" > "is related to".
- Keep relations SHORT: 1-4 words maximum. "co-developed" not "co-developed the CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing technology for". The description field carries the detail.
- Do NOT extract trivially generic relationships (e.g. "Entity A exists" or "Entity B is a concept").
- Do NOT extract bare years (e.g. "2008", "2016") or date mentions as Event entities. Only extract named events (e.g. "2008 Financial Crisis", "Apollo 11").
- Do NOT extract species names, geographic features, or other nouns that are mentioned but do not participate in meaningful relationships.
- Every entity you list MUST appear in at least one relationship, and every relationship MUST reference entities from your entity list.
